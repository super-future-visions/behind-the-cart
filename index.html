The biggest number is what’s missing

97% of about 1,000 companies reviewed did not publicly report assessing the environmental impact of their AI systems.

Source: AI Company Data Initiative, via Lab Manager. It measures what companies share publicly, and mostly covers companies that use AI.

The biggest tech companies are no different. None of the five we checked reports a full figure for how much of its electricity goes to AI.

How open are they?

We scored how much each company publicly shares about the energy, water and emissions of its data centers: 2 points for full details, 1 for partial, 0 if we found nothing. This measures openness, not how big a footprint is.

What full marks would require
  • Total electricity: every year, in absolute units, for the whole company and for data centers, with the method and boundary stated.
  • AI-specific electricity: training and everyday use reported separately, with the method.
  • Energy per prompt: for flagship models and more than text, with the method, the date, and independent review.
  • Emissions by local grid: location-based and market-based figures side by side.
  • Water: consumption and withdrawal, totals and by site, with water stress noted and indirect water at power plants included.
  • Each data center: electricity and water per site, including leased and third-party hosted capacity.
  • Independent checking: third-party assurance on every disclosed metric, including the AI-specific ones.

By this standard, no company in our sample would earn full marks on the AI-specific questions today, and our grid is more lenient than this standard.

Electricity use over time

AI and cloud services run in data centers that use huge amounts of electricity. Compare what the biggest operators report, year by year. This chart covers electricity. The grid below covers everything else, including what is missing.

Read with care. These are the latest figures companies and the IEA have published, not live meters. Google and Meta report data-center electricity. Microsoft reports company-wide electricity by fiscal year (July to June). Amazon does not publish a total that we could find. Apple reports electricity for its owned data centers (2022 to 2024 in our data), not including colocation or rented cloud capacity. None of them separates AI from other cloud work, so AI’s share can’t be read directly from this data. Published estimates of electricity per AI prompt vary widely: one 2024 analysis cited about 2.9 Wh per ChatGPT request versus about 0.3 Wh per Google search, while more recent company disclosures report much lower figures for median text prompts. How these are measured is debated.

Sources and what needs checking

Google and Meta series: Datacenters on Map, which cites Google’s 2026 Environmental Report and Meta’s sustainability reports. Meta’s own 2025 Environmental Data Index lists company-wide electricity of 7.2 TWh in 2020 rising to 18.4 TWh in 2024, and its data-center series matches the chart (we checked it directly). Worldwide series (IEA): Datacenters on Map; the 2025 value is an estimate. Microsoft: 2026 Environmental Data Fact Sheet (company-wide electricity, fiscal years 2020 to 2025, read directly). Apple: DataCenterDynamics on Apple’s Environmental Progress Reports (data-center electricity, 2022 to 2024) and The Planet Brief on the 2026 report. Google and worldwide series are secondary sources, so check them against the original reports before launch. Another source lists slightly different Google values for 2024 (30.8 TWh), which shows how definitions differ.

The same electricity, counted two ways

Companies can report emissions from electricity two ways. The market-based method credits purchased renewable certificates, so it can look very low. The location-based method counts the emissions of the local power grid where the electricity is used. Reporting both lets you see the difference.

  • Microsoft, fiscal 2025: 12.0 million tonnes CO₂e location-based versus 2.7 million market-based, about 4.4 times as much. In fiscal 2024 it was 10.0 versus 0.26 million, about 38 times.
  • Google, 2024: 11.3 million versus 3.1 million tonnes, about 3.7 times (read through a policy analysis).
  • Meta, 2024: 5.97 million tonnes location-based versus 1,358 tonnes market-based, about 4,400 times as much, from its own data index.
  • Amazon, 2025: 21.8 million versus 3.7 million tonnes, about 5.8 times as much, from an Ernst & Young review schedule. Company-wide, not AWS only.
  • Apple, 2025: 1.21 million tonnes location-based versus 1,100 tonnes market-based, about 1,100 times as much, according to The Planet Brief reading its assurance statement. Apple sources a very large share of its electricity through renewable contracts, so the market-based number is tiny.
  • Meta’s Llama 3.1 training, 2024: 11,390 tonnes CO₂e location-based versus 0 market-based, because Meta matches its electricity with renewable energy contracts (from the model card).

Microsoft’s market-based figure rose sharply in fiscal 2025 after it stopped buying certain short-term renewable certificates in February 2025, according to its data fact sheet. Companies that report only the market-based number hide this difference.
